Talent — Costs Behind the Work
Every initiative and focus area carries a cost — both labor and non-labor. Atlassian Talent connects people, roles, and allocations to the strategic work, so leadership can see the real cost of delivering against a focus area alongside its funding treatment (CAPEX/OPEX).

Intake — Where Ideas Begin
Ideas can come from users, product leads, or big company initiatives that may have an impact on product. Jira Product Discovery captures these from multiple channels and gives product teams a structured way to evaluate what comes in.

Strategy — Executive Planning
At the strategic level, we have initiatives that could be capitalized — new things we're doing. But we also want to keep track of our KTLO (Keep The Lights On) work. You can keep that CAPEX/OPEX distinction at the executive level and simply connect to delivery work without having any type of impact on how the teams operate.

OKRs — Connecting Goals to Work
Objectives and Key Results can be attached to initiatives. They can also be attached to work. This creates a direct line of sight between what leadership cares about and what teams are delivering.

Products — Lifecycle Management
Products go through a lifecycle where they are assessed, scored, organized, and then eventually connected to the delivery pipeline. Product Discovery enables you to allow a multitude of people to offer assessments and visually see what's happening within the product roadmap.

Product Roadmap — Share Externally
The product roadmap enables you to share roadmaps publicly. This can help you with people who don't have a Jira Product Discovery license but need to understand where the product is going — stakeholders, partners, customers, or anyone outside the immediate product team.

Jira — The Delivery Engine
Whether it's brand new work or new products, everything needs to get connected to teams who are doing the work. Teams have various structures. Work can connect at any level — so governance is required to ensure that connections happen at the right level.
Jira

Portfolio Epics — Strategy Meets Delivery
For example, you may want to connect high-level work items directly to company initiatives and allow the parent-child relationship built into Jira to describe what's happening on that initiative. A Portfolio Epic spans a year, breaks into month-long Epics, and further into week-long Stories.

Analytics — The Leadership View
You can bring all of these things together for leadership views that can be sliced and diced — by funding (CAPEX/OPEX), by work item, by strategy (initiative), by product, or by goal (OKR). This is where executive visibility lives.

Jira Plans — Cross-Cutting Roadmap
Jira Plans enables you to see across goals, OKRs, strategic items, and products — so that the same work item, for example a feature, can describe its connection to all of these objects. This offers a Gantt chart view where you can navigate from the top to the bottom of the hierarchy.

The Structure of Strategy to Delivery Work
Fundamentally, organizations have structures similar to this. There are leaders who are trying to create revenue — they have a unique view of the world. There are product people who are trying to contain and maintain stability in their products — they also have a unique view of the world. The delivery teams are working on tasks that answer these unique questions, and often the same piece of work is being asked to answer to multiple questions. The Atlassian suite of tools considers this issue and facilitates understanding across all three levels.

The Simplest Model — All Jira
The Why level — the reason why we're doing it — can live at the top of the work item hierarchy, usually in an Initiative. The ideas about what and why we are doing something, and the ability to answer those ideas, fundamentally rely on a parent-child spine in Jira. It's possible to identify how these questions will get answered using a simple Jira spine and custom fields. This box represents the simplest implementation — everything lives in one tool, with the "Product" field on the Feature/Epic connecting delivery work back to the product context without requiring a separate product management layer.
